Chapter 4 — Differentiation and Mixed Ability

35 prompts

Differentiation fails when it becomes three separate lessons running in one room. These prompts keep one destination and change the route: same objective, same final discussion, different ways in.

Worth knowing before you start

Never name a student. Describe the need — "students reading about two years below grade level" — not the person.

Ask for neutral names for the routes. "Route A, B, C" works; anything that reads as a ranking will be decoded by the class within one lesson.

Push back if the extension is just more questions. Extension means harder thinking, not extra volume, and several prompts here say so explicitly.

Whatever accommodation you supply must survive into the draft. Check that it did.

Example — an editorial illustration, not a logged AI run

Keep the goal: explain matter inputs and products with light as energy. Support: colour-independent arrow labels and a frame, Water and carbon dioxide are used to... .
