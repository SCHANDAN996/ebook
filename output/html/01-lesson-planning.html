Chapter 1 — Lesson Planning

60 prompts

Most lesson planning goes wrong in the same place: you start listing activities

before you have decided what would count as proof that the lesson worked. These

prompts push the evidence to the front — what will you see, in the room, that

tells you they got it — and build the lesson backward from there.

Worth knowing before you start

Give it the real constraints — 50 minutes, 26 students, no lab this week. Vague

input produces a plan for a class that does not exist.

If a standard has to be followed, paste its exact wording. Do not ask the model

to recall a standard code; it will produce something that looks right.

Treat every suggested timing as optimistic. Handing out paper takes four minutes.
