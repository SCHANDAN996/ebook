Before you use any prompt in this book

Read this once. It applies to every prompt in every chapter.

The one-minute version

AI writes a first draft. You are the teacher. Nothing in this book produces

something that goes to a student, a family, or a permanent record without you

reading it first.

Never paste these into an AI tool

  • Student names, initials or ID numbers
  • Contact details for students or families
  • Medical information, diagnoses, or protected plans
  • Confidential incident or safeguarding records
  • Any story detailed enough to identify one child

Write "a Year 5 student" or "Student A" instead.

Taking the name out is not the same as making it anonymous. A class of 28 with
one wheelchair user is identifiable without any name at all. If the detail is
what makes the request work, that is exactly the detail to leave out.

Use whatever AI tool your school has approved. If your school has not approved

one, ask before putting any school information into any of them.

What AI may write, and what it may not

It may invent: teaching activities, practice questions, worked examples,

fictional scenarios, sentence starters, structures and sequences.

It must never invent:

Not thisWhy
What a student said or didYou were there. It wasn't.
Research findings or quotationsIt will produce plausible fake citations
Standards, codes, or policy wordingCheck the real document
Dates, approvals, meeting recordsThese are factual records
Test scores or measured resultsThese are evidence, not text

If something you need is missing, the prompt is written to ask you rather

than fill the gap. When it asks, answer — do not let it guess.

Judgements that stay with you

AI does not decide a final grade, a placement, a disciplinary outcome, or

anything to do with safeguarding. It can help you draft the words. The decision

is yours, and so is the responsibility.

Never let it infer a diagnosis, a home situation, a motivation, an identity, or

a fixed ability level. It cannot know these, and a confident sentence about them

is still a guess.

Keep any accommodation or constraint you supplied. If you said a student has

extra time, that must survive into the draft.

Two habits worth building

Check the maths and the facts yourself. If you ask the model to check its

own work it will say it checked. That is not verification.

Read the draft as the person receiving it. A parent email that reads fine to

you at 9pm may not read fine to a family at 8am.

If a request touches harm or danger

Stop. Do not work it through with an AI tool. Follow your school's safeguarding

or emergency procedure. That process exists for exactly this moment.

Paste this once, at the start of your chat

Instead of repeating rules in every prompt, paste this at the top of a new

conversation. Everything after it inherits the rules.

You are helping a classroom teacher draft materials.

Rules for this whole conversation:
- I will not give you any student's name, ID, or identifying details, and you
  must not ask for them.
- You may invent activities, examples and fictional scenarios. Never invent
  student observations, research, quotations, standards, policies, dates,
  approvals or measured results.
- If something essential is missing, ask me. Do not fill the gap yourself.
- Keep any constraint or accommodation I give you.
- Do not infer a diagnosis, home situation, motivation, identity, or fixed
  ability level.
- Separate what I told you, what you are proposing, and what you are unsure of.
- End each answer with a short list of what I should check before using it.

Confirm you have these, then wait for my first task.

Then paste any prompt from any chapter.
