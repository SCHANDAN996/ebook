# How to use this book

## The short version

1. Find the prompt for what you are actually doing right now.
2. Copy everything between **COPY FROM HERE** and **TO HERE**.
3. Paste it into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini.
4. Fill in the parts in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` with your real details.
5. Read what comes back before anyone else does.

That is the whole method. Nothing to install, nothing to learn first.

---

## Two files for every chapter

Each chapter comes as a **PDF** and an **HTML page**, with the same content.

| | PDF | HTML page |
|---|---|---|
| Copy button on every prompt | no | **yes** |
| Works offline / prints | **yes** | no |
| Search inside | yes | yes |
| On a phone | fine | **better** |

A PDF cannot carry a working copy button — the feature exists in the format, but
no browser or phone viewer runs it. So **use the HTML page when you are actually
working**, and keep the PDF for printing or reading offline.

## Filling in the brackets

Every prompt has parts like `[GRADE_SUBJECT]` or `[UNIT_TOPIC]`. Replace the
whole thing, brackets included.

> `[GRADE_SUBJECT: exact age/grade and subject]`
> becomes
> `7th grade science`

**Be specific.** "7th grade science, mixed ability, 26 students, 50-minute lesson,
no lab access this week" gets you something usable. "Science" gets you something
generic.

If a prompt asks for something you genuinely do not have, say so in the chat
rather than inventing it. The prompts are written to ask you back.

---

## Getting a better result

**Answer its questions.** If it comes back asking for the assessment date or the
reading level, the prompt is working correctly. Answer, and the next draft will
be much closer.

**Ask for a change instead of starting again.** "Same plan, but guided practice
is too long for 15 minutes" is faster than a fresh prompt.

**One task at a time.** These prompts are deliberately narrow. Asking for a
lesson plan, a worksheet and a parent email in one go gets you three mediocre
things.

## What the sections mean

**When you need this** — the situation, so you can tell two similar prompts apart.

**Fill in** — what you need to hand before you start.

**COPY FROM HERE / TO HERE** — the prompt. Copy all of it.

**Before you send it** — what usually goes wrong on *this* kind of task. Short,
and specific to this prompt.

**Example** — where included, an illustration of the shape of a good answer. It
is an editorial illustration, not a logged AI run.

---

## Three things this book will not do

**It will not know your students.** Nothing here should be given identifying
details. See the safety page.

**It will not check your subject facts.** Verify content and calculations
yourself, especially anything a student will be marked on.

**It will not decide anything.** Grades, placements, behavior outcomes and
anything touching child protection stay with you and your school's process.
