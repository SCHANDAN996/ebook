# Extend the toolkit responsibly

These short adaptation recipes extend existing tools; they are not extra counted prompts.

## Early literacy and numeracy

Adapt LP-008 for one teacher-selected phoneme-grapheme correspondence using a verified word
list from the taught sequence. Ask for oral rehearsal, a model and a short check; independently
check every pronunciation and spelling. Do not ask AI to invent a reading diagnosis. For
early numeracy, adapt SD-003 with counters and quantities appropriate to the taught range.

## Co-teaching and approved accommodations

Use DF-001 and AD-008 to allocate teacher roles around the same learning goal. Supply access
requirements without a learner's diagnosis or protected plan. Required accommodations are
not automatically faded when optional scaffolds are removed. Coordinate changes through the
authorized team, not through the AI output.

## Responsible student AI use

Adapt WA-011: give a clearly labelled fictional AI answer containing a teacher-verified error.
Learners identify the claim, locate evidence, correct the answer and explain what remains
uncertain. Do not accuse a learner of AI misuse from style or an automated detector alone.
Use AS-007 for process evidence such as explanation, revision and an in-class response.

## Multilingual families and translation

Use PC-027 to prepare plain source text and a terminology list. A competent human checks
the translated message, especially dates, negation, costs and required actions. Back-translation
can expose problems but is not proof of equivalence. Confirm the family's preferred channel locally.

## Attendance and catch-up

Combine PC-010 with LP-047. Distinguish absent, missing, late and not-yet-marked work. Offer
one feasible in-class catch-up route; do not assume home internet, adult teaching or a reason
for absence. Refer welfare concerns through the school's approved process.

## Remote and hybrid access

Use LP-046 and WA-033 to make an offline equivalent. Specify device limits, bandwidth,
response format and accessible alternatives. Check that an offline learner can demonstrate
the same goal without printing, paid software or an adult acting as the teacher.

## Computing, languages, music and practical subjects

For computing, adapt SD-004 to a short teacher-verified program with expected inputs/outputs;
execute code in a safe environment before assigning it. For languages, supply verified
vocabulary and a communicative goal to WA-003; review idiom and pronunciation. For music,
adapt SD-013 with a teacher-approved rhythm and accessible performance options. For health
or practical activity, use only approved materials and safety rules; do not generate individualized
medical, exercise or rehabilitation prescriptions.

## Source literacy, culture and feedback

Use SD-010 and SD-011 to compare provenance, independence and evidence quality. Ask whose
perspective is missing without treating all sources as equally reliable. Use AS-023 for learner
self-assessment, then compare the learner's evidence with the teacher's evidence privately.
Use PC-003's listening structure for a student conference without repurposing confidential family notes.
