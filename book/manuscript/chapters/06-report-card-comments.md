# Chapter 6 — Report Card Comments

**30 prompts**

Twenty-eight comments in one sitting is the job nobody plans for and everybody
dreads. These prompts do the drafting so you can do the judging — every comment
tied to something you actually recorded, with one next step the student can act
on.

## What's in here

| Group | Prompts | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Comment banks | 10 | Build once, reuse every term |
| Strengths and next steps | 7 | The core shape of a good comment |
| Subject-specific comments | 7 | Language that fits the subject |
| Bulk drafts from evidence | 4 | A whole class in one pass |
| Tone and rewriting | 3 | Honest, but survivable to read |

## Worth knowing before you start

**Use initials or "Student A".** These comments go into a permanent record. The
drafting should not put a child's name into an AI tool.

Give it your real evidence — scores, dates, things you wrote down. A comment
built on nothing reads like a comment built on nothing.

Do not let it invent a strength to balance the structure. A manufactured
compliment is the fastest way for a family to stop trusting the rest.

Vary the openings. Twenty-eight comments that all begin the same way are
recognisable as generated, and parents compare.

### RC-001 · Build an evidence-safe comment bank
*All grades*

**When you need this**
Build it once in January and use it every term after.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Build a report comment bank for [GRADE] [SUBJECT].

Give me 6 comments for each of these bands: excelling, secure, approaching,
and inconsistent evidence.

Every comment must contain:
- [NAME] once, at the start
- [SPECIFIC EVIDENCE] — a blank I fill with something real from my records
- One next step the student can act on themselves

Rules:
- No comment may be sendable without filling the evidence blank. Write them so
  the gap is obvious
- Describe what the student did, never what kind of learner they are
- No praise for effort, attitude, behaviour or personality unless I add it
- Vary the openings across the bank — list them so I can check no two match
- Nothing that predicts or promises future performance

Then tell me which band is hardest to write honestly, and why.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Could any comment be sent without filling the evidence blank? Tighten it.
- Do the openings genuinely differ?

### RC-004 · Create comment starters for four levels of demonstrated mastery
*All grades*

**When you need this**
Openings that describe the learning rather than rank the child.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write comment openings for [GRADE] [SUBJECT] at four levels of demonstrated
mastery.

Give me 5 openings per level. Each must:
- Describe what the student has demonstrated, in the work
- Be followed naturally by a specific piece of evidence
- Avoid comparison with other students or with a class average
- Avoid any word implying fixed ability — bright, weak, able, gifted, slow,
  natural, struggles

For the lowest band especially: the opening must be honest without being a
verdict on the child. Show me the difference between an honest opening and a
discouraging one, with an example of each.

Then tell me which openings would sound wrong if a family read four of them in
a row from different teachers.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Would you be comfortable if a family compared four of these?
- Any fixed-ability words left?

### RC-005 · Build modular strength, evidence and next-step sentence parts
*All grades*

**When you need this**
Mix and match, so thirty comments do not read as one.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Build modular comment parts for [GRADE] [SUBJECT] that I can combine.

Give me three separate lists:
- 8 strength openings
- 8 evidence connectors, each leading into a [SPECIFIC EXAMPLE] blank
- 8 next-step closings

Every combination must produce a grammatical, natural comment. Show me four
assembled examples using different combinations.

Rules:
- No part may contain a fact — the facts come from my blanks
- Next steps must be things the student can do, not things that will happen to
  them
- No closing may promise a result

Then tell me which combinations sound wrong together, so I avoid them at 10pm.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Assemble three at random. Do they read naturally?
- Do any parts carry a claim you have not verified?

### RC-006 · Create a comment bank that avoids fixed-ability labels
*All grades*

**When you need this**
Language that describes this term, not the child's ceiling.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Build a comment bank for [GRADE] [SUBJECT] that describes learning as it stands
now, not as a fixed trait.

Banned outright: bright, able, gifted, weak, low, high, slow, natural,
struggles with, is not a [subject] person, lacks ability, and any comparison to
peers.

Give me:
- 12 comments describing current demonstrated learning
- For each, the fixed-ability phrasing it replaces, so I can see the swap
- The three phrases teachers reach for most that sound neutral but are not
- How to describe a student who is genuinely far behind, honestly, without
  making it sound permanent

That last one is the point of this prompt. Do not avoid it by being vague.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Is the far-behind comment honest, or has it gone vague to be kind?
- Check your own habitual phrases against the list of three.

### RC-007 · Write concise comments within a strict character limit
*All grades*

**When you need this**
The reporting system allows 250 characters and no argument.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write [GRADE] [SUBJECT] report comments within [NUMBER] characters including
spaces.

Evidence: [PASTE WHAT YOU HAVE — no names]

Rules:
- Count the characters and state the count after each comment
- Cut adverbs and hedging before cutting substance
- The next step survives the cut. If something has to go, it is not that
- No abbreviations a family would have to decode
- Still specific — a short comment is not an excuse for a generic one

Give me each comment with its character count, and tell me what you dropped to
fit.

If the evidence genuinely cannot be said in [NUMBER] characters without becoming
meaningless, say so rather than producing something empty.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Verify the character counts yourself. They are often wrong.
- Did the next step survive?

### RC-008 · Create neutral pronoun variants without changing the evidence
*All grades*

**When you need this**
One comment, correct for any student, no rewriting at 11pm.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Rewrite these [GRADE] [SUBJECT] comments so pronouns are not baked in.

Give me each comment in two forms:
- Using the student's name where a pronoun would go, so it works for anyone

- Do not add or remove meaning in the rewrite

and give me your best alternative.

COMMENTS:
[PASTE THEM]
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Read one aloud. Does the name repeat too often?
- Is the evidence identical across versions?

### RC-009 · Build a comment bank with visible placeholders for supporting evidence
*All grades*

**When you need this**
So the blank cannot be skipped when you are tired.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Build a [GRADE] [SUBJECT] comment bank where the evidence blanks are impossible
to miss.

For each comment:
- Put placeholders in a form that stands out visibly, such as [[LIKE THIS]]
- Every comment needs at least one evidence placeholder and one next-step
  placeholder
- Write a one-line rule beside each placeholder saying what belongs there — the
  kind of evidence, not an example I might just copy

Give me 10 comments across the range.

Then give me the two-line check I run before submitting: what to search for to
confirm no placeholder survived into a final comment.

Do not fill any placeholder with a plausible example. That is exactly how an
invented detail ends up in a permanent record.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Search the final file for `[[`. Every one must be gone.
- Has any placeholder been helpfully pre-filled?

### RC-010 · Audit a comment bank for repetition, vagueness and invented claims
*All grades*

**When you need this**
Before it goes into use for three years.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Audit this [GRADE] [SUBJECT] comment bank.

Tell me:
- Which comments are the same comment in different words
- Which contain no actual information — quote the empty phrases
- Which make a claim that cannot be evidenced from classroom work
- Which describe personality, effort or attitude rather than learning
- Which imply a fixed ability
- Which would sound identical if a family compared two children's reports

Then tell me:
- The comments to delete entirely
- The single vaguest phrase in the whole bank

BANK:
[PASTE IT]
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Delete what it said to delete. A bank of 30 good comments beats 60 mixed.
- Is the vaguest phrase one you use often?

### RC-002 · Turn evidence into a balanced report comment
*All grades*

**When you need this**
The core shape: what they can do, what comes next, both evidenced.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write a report comment for a [GRADE] [SUBJECT] student.

Evidence I have: [LIST IT — scores, observations, dates, work. No names.]

Structure:
- A strength, with the evidence stated
- A next step, specific and achievable this term
- Nothing else

Rules:
- Every factual clause must trace to something I gave you. Mark any clause you
  cannot trace, rather than including it
- Do not manufacture a strength to balance the comment. If my evidence shows no
  clear strength, say so and tell me what to look for
- The next step is something the student does, not something I do
- No prediction, no promise, no praise for effort I did not describe
- [NUMBER] sentences

Give me the comment, plus a note on which clause rests on the thinnest evidence.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Trace every clause to your own records.
- If it said the evidence shows no strength, do not add one.

### RC-011 · Write a strength statement tied to a specific learning objective
*All grades*

**When you need this**
"Doing well" means nothing. This says in what.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write a strength statement for a [GRADE] [SUBJECT] student.

Objective: [PASTE IT]
What the student did: [THE EVIDENCE — no names]

The statement must name the objective in family-readable language and point at
what the student actually produced.

Give me:
- The strength statement, one or two sentences
- The same statement without the jargon, if my objective contains any
- What makes this a strength rather than simply completing the task
- A version for a student whose strength is real but narrow — secure in one
  part of the objective and not the rest

Do not broaden the claim beyond my evidence. One good piece of work is not a
consistent pattern, and should not be described as one.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Has one instance been described as a pattern?
- Would a family understand the objective as written?

### RC-012 · Convert an error pattern into one achievable next step
*All grades*

**When you need this**
One next step. Not four, and not a wish.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Turn this [GRADE] [SUBJECT] error pattern into a single next step:

[DESCRIBE WHAT KEEPS HAPPENING IN THE WORK — no names]

The next step must be:
- One thing, not a list
- Something the student can start without me
- Achievable this term, not aspirational
- Written so the student understands what to do differently

Give me:
- The next step, one sentence
- The same thing said to the student directly
- What I do in class to support it
- How I would know in three weeks whether it worked

Do not write "should continue to practise" or "needs to focus more". Those are
not next steps. If the pattern needs teaching rather than practice, say so.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Could the student start this tomorrow, alone?
- Is it one thing?

### RC-013 · Build a balanced comment from assessment and classroom evidence
*All grades*

**When you need this**
The test score and what you see daily do not agree.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write a report comment combining assessment and classroom evidence for a
[GRADE] [SUBJECT] student.

Assessment evidence: [SCORES, DATES]

Where these disagree, say so rather than averaging them into a bland middle.

Give me:
- The comment
- Which evidence you weighted more, and why

- What I should say if a family asks why the comment and the grade differ

If the two sources disagree strongly enough that I should gather more evidence
before reporting, tell me that instead of writing the comment.
```

- Do you agree with which evidence it weighted?
- Can you answer the family question it prepared you for?

### RC-014 · Write a comment when evidence is limited or inconsistent
*All grades*

**When you need this**
Long absence, arrived late in the term, or the evidence simply is not there.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write a [GRADE] [SUBJECT] report comment where I have limited evidence.

What I have: [LIST IT HONESTLY, INCLUDING GAPS AND WHY — no names]

Rules:
- Say plainly what the comment is based on, and over what period
- Do not extrapolate from a small sample into a general judgement
- Do not fill the gap with attendance commentary, effort, or behaviour
- Do not imply the student is behind if I do not have evidence for that
- Give a next step only if the evidence supports one; otherwise say what I will
  look for next term

Give me the comment and one line I could add explaining the limited basis
without sounding defensive.

Tell me if the honest answer is that I cannot write a meaningful comment yet.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Has thin evidence quietly become a judgement?
- Is the limitation stated plainly?

### RC-015 · Create a learner-friendly next step that families can understand
*All grades*

**When you need this**
The next step should mean something to the child it is about.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Rewrite this next step so a [GRADE] student and their family both understand it:

[PASTE IT]

Give me:
- The next step in student-facing words
- The same for a family, with no subject jargon
- One concrete example of what doing it looks like
- One thing a family could do to help, taking under ten minutes and requiring
  no subject knowledge

Rules:
- Do not ask a family to teach anything
- Do not assume internet, a printer, purchased resources, or an adult free at a
  fixed time
- Keep the demand identical to the original — plainer words, same expectation

Flag anything in the original the student could not act on alone.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Does the family suggestion require any teaching? Cut it if so.
- Is the demand unchanged?

### RC-016 · Add an instructional strategy without promising a future result
*All grades*

**When you need this**
Saying what you will do, without guaranteeing what will happen.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Add to this [GRADE] [SUBJECT] comment a line about what I will do in class:

[PASTE THE COMMENT]
What I plan to do: [DESCRIBE IT]

Rules:
- State the action, not the expected outcome
- No promise, no prediction, no "this will help them to"
- No timescale I cannot guarantee
- It must be something I will actually do, not a general intention
- One sentence

Give me three versions at different levels of specificity, and tell me which is
safest to put in a permanent record.

Then flag any sentence in my original comment that already promises a result.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Will you actually do it? It is now on a record.
- Any promises left in the original?

### RC-017 · Audit a report comment using a sentence-by-sentence fact trace
*All grades*

**When you need this**
The last check before it becomes permanent.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Trace this [GRADE] [SUBJECT] report comment against my evidence.

COMMENT: [PASTE IT]
EVIDENCE: [PASTE WHAT YOU HAVE]

For each sentence, tell me:
- Which piece of evidence supports it
- Whether it is fully supported, partly supported, or unsupported
- Where it generalises beyond what the evidence shows
- Whether it describes learning, or effort, behaviour or personality
- Whether a family could challenge it and be right

Then give me:
- The sentence to cut
- The sentence to soften
- Anything the evidence supports that the comment does not mention

Do not accept a sentence as supported because it sounds reasonable.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Cut what it said to cut.
- Anything unsupported goes, however true it feels.

### RC-018 · Write a mathematics comment about concepts, procedures and reasoning
*Grades 3-5, 6-8, 9-12*

**When you need this**
"Good at maths" hides which of the three is actually strong.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write a [GRADE] mathematics report comment.

Evidence: [WHAT THE WORK SHOWS — no names]

Separate three things, because a student can be strong in one and not the others:
- Conceptual understanding — do they know what it means
- Procedural fluency — can they carry it out reliably
- Reasoning and explanation — can they justify and communicate it

Give me:
- The comment, naming which of the three is the strength and which is the next
  step
- The sentence a family will understand, since these three sound alike to
  non-teachers
- What a student who is procedurally fluent but conceptually shaky looks like,
  so I can check I have read my evidence correctly

If my evidence only covers one of the three, say so rather than commenting on
all three.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Does your evidence actually cover what the comment claims?
- Would a family understand the distinction as written?

### RC-019 · Write a science comment about investigation, evidence and explanation
*Grades 3-5, 6-8, 9-12*

**When you need this**
Practical work and written explanation are different skills.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write a [GRADE] science report comment.

Evidence: [WHAT THE WORK AND PRACTICALS SHOW — no names]

Distinguish:
- Working scientifically — planning, controlling variables, measuring, recording
- Handling evidence — reading data, spotting patterns, noting limitations
- Explaining — using scientific ideas to account for what was observed

Give me:
- The comment, naming which of these the evidence supports
- The distinction between a student who follows a method well and one who
  understands why the method is designed that way
- A next step for a student strong in practical work and weak in written
  explanation, that is not simply "write more"

Do not credit understanding from a neat write-up alone.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Are you crediting understanding, or presentation?
- Is the next step more than "write more"?

### RC-020 · Write an English language arts comment about reading and writing evidence
*Grades 3-5, 6-8, 9-12*

**When you need this**
Reading and writing are separate, and so is talking about them.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write a [GRADE] English report comment.

Evidence: [READING AND WRITING EVIDENCE — no names]

Keep reading and writing separate. A student can comprehend well and write
poorly, and the comment should not blur them.

For reading, distinguish: decoding, literal comprehension, inference, and
response to a text.
For writing, distinguish: ideas, structure, sentence control, and technical
accuracy.

Give me:
- The comment, naming specifically where the strength and next step sit
- Why technical accuracy should not dominate the comment if the ideas are strong
- A next step for a student whose ideas outrun their sentence control

Do not describe a student as a "reluctant" or "confident" reader or writer.
Those are dispositions, not evidence.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Has technical accuracy swamped everything else?
- Any disposition language left?

### RC-023 · Write a physical education comment using observable skill evidence
*Grades 3-5, 6-8, 9-12*

**When you need this**
Comment on the learning, not on the body.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Write a [GRADE] physical education report comment.

Evidence: [OBSERVED SKILLS, DECISION-MAKING, PARTICIPATION IN LEARNING — no names]

limited but whose understanding and decision-making are strong.

Flag any phrase in this that could read as a comment on the child rather than
the learning.
```

**Before you send it**
- Read it as the child. Is any of it about their body?
- Would it work for a student with a restriction?

*All grades*

**When you need this**
Testing your evidence format before you use it on a real class.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Create a worked example of bulk comment drafting for [GRADE] [SUBJECT].

Give me:
- The fictional table
- The 6 comments
- For each, which column of the table produced which clause
- The evidence format that made drafting easiest, so I know what to record
  during term

This is a template test, not real reporting. Do not present the fictional
students as real.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Does the recommended evidence format match what you can realistically collect?
- Are the six genuinely different in shape?

### RC-026 · Generate bulk comments without mixing one learner's evidence with another
*All grades*

**When you need this**
Thirty comments in one pass, and nothing may cross between them.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Draft report comments from this evidence table for [GRADE] [SUBJECT].

[PASTE YOUR TABLE — use initials or Student A, B, C. No full names.]

Rules — the first is the one that matters:
- Every clause in a comment must come from that student's own row. Never carry a
  detail, a phrase or an achievement from one row into another
- If a row is thin, write a shorter comment. Do not borrow to fill it
- Vary structure and openings across the set
- Mark any clause you could not source to the row

Give me the comments, and afterwards a checklist: for each student, the row
facts used and any facts unused.

Then flag any two comments similar enough that a family comparing them would
notice.
```

**TO HERE**

**Before you send it**
- Check the used-facts list against your table, row by row.
- This is where evidence leaks between students. Do not skip this check.

### RC-029 · Replace harsh or vague judgments with verified learning evidence
*All grades*

**When you need this**
The comment says something about the child that the evidence does not.

**COPY FROM HERE**

```text
Rewrite this [GRADE] [SUBJECT] comment, replacing judgement with evidence.
```
